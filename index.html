About

I work on generative AI for chip thermal and power simulation, and on using it to drive more efficient thermal management. Thermal analysis of 3D-stacked chiplets currently relies on solvers that take hours per design point, which puts it out of reach of the design loop it needs to inform. My research replaces parts of that computation with learned models.

Before joining Minnesota I worked on device reliability and compact modeling at Peking University. I have 11 publications to date, 4 as first or co-first author, including a co-first-authored paper in Nature.

Research

Generative models for full-chip thermal and power maps

Reconstructing full-resolution temperature and power maps from a small number of on-die sensors and performance counters, with calibrated uncertainty, so that thermal information is available at runtime rather than after an offline solve.

Learned surrogates for 3D-IC and chiplet stacks

Graph-based models that transfer across floorplans and stack configurations instead of being refit for every new design.

Device reliability and compact modeling

Earlier work on neural-network methods for extracting hot-carrier degradation model parameters, along with self-heating and electrothermal simulation for advanced nodes.
