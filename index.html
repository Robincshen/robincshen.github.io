Research

Thermal simulation is the bottleneck.
I'm replacing it with learned models.

Chiplet stacks trap heat between dies, and the finite-element solvers that predict it take hours per design point. That cost sits directly in the inner loop of floorplanning, power delivery, and runtime thermal management. My work asks how much of that solver you can learn — and how far you can get from the sparse, noisy signals a real chip actually reports.

Diffusion models

Full-chip maps from sparse sensors

A cascaded conditional diffusion framework that reconstructs full-resolution temperature and power maps from a handful of on-die sensors and performance counters, with calibrated uncertainty on every pixel.

SiliDiff · under review

Graph neural networks

Surrogates for 3D-IC stacks

Treating a chiplet stack as a graph of thermal nodes so a single trained model transfers across floorplans and stack configurations, instead of being refit per design.

Ongoing

Where this started

Learned compact models for device reliability

Neural networks that extract hot-carrier degradation model parameters in seconds instead of hours, plus self-heating and electrothermal work on backside power delivery. Same idea as the thermal work, one length scale down.
